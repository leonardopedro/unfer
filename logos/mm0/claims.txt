# The math fragment's claim corpus — every line is a sentence the pipeline
# certifies, with the verdict it must reach. `logos mm0 claims` runs this file
# and fails if any line reaches a different verdict, so a change that makes the
# pipeline prove something false (or stop proving something true) is caught here
# rather than by a reader of the certificates.
#
# `true:`  — closed claim, must be certified by a proof of `teq L R`
# `false:` — closed claim, must be refuted (`teq L R ⟹ tfals`)
# `proved:`— open (quantified) claim, must be closed by rewriting (or a rule)
#
# Keep the numbers small: the proof of a sum is as long as the right operand, so
# 30 + 12 is 12 `add_s` steps — cheap, and the corpus stays readable.

# ── closed sums ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
true: two plus three equals five
true: 1 + 1 = 2
true: zero plus zero equals zero
true: twelve plus eight equals twenty
true: 30 + 12 = 42
false: two plus three equals six
false: two plus two equals five
false: 30 + 12 = 41
false: zero plus zero equals one

# ── closed products ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
true: two times three equals six
true: five times five equals twenty five
true: 7 * 0 = 0
true: 12 times 1 equals 12
false: two times three equals five
false: five times five equals twenty four

# ── the scale boundary: the first claim whose numeral overflowed the checker ──
# `99 * 99` builds the numeral `ts^9801 tz`, deep enough that mm0's checkers
# (which recurse over term structure) blew the default 8MB stack and aborted
# with `thread 'main' has overflowed its stack` before giving any verdict.
# The tools are now spawned with room to fit (emit.rs `tool_cmd`), so both
# directions certify. One large line keeps that fix honest; the rest of the
# corpus stays small so its certificates stay readable.
true: 99 times 99 equals 9801
false: 99 times 99 equals 9802

# ── nesting: the operator that commits first ─────────────────────────────────
true: two plus three times four equals fourteen
true: (two plus three) times four equals twenty
false: two plus three times four equals twenty
true: two times two plus two times two equals eight

# ── the grammar's other spellings of the same operators ──────────────────────
# `and` reads as `+`, `multiplied by` as `*`, `equal to` as `=`, and
# capitalization does not matter: each line below is the *same certificate* as
# its plain-spelling sibling above (theorem names are content-addressed on the
# goal), so this section checks that the alternate spellings really lower to
# the same claim rather than to something merely similar.
true: two and three equals five
true: two plus three equal to five
true: Two Plus Three Equals Five
true: three multiplied by four equals twelve

# ── numeral words: teens, hyphens, and mixing bases on one side ──────────────
true: 17 + twenty five = 42
true: seventeen plus five equals twenty two
true: twenty-five plus zero equals 25

# ── the successor: the constructor the upstream Peano theorems are stated in ─
true: suc 4 equals 5
true: 5 equals suc 4
true: suc (two plus three) equals six
false: suc 4 equals 4
proved: for all a: a plus one equals suc a
proved: for all a: suc a plus zero equals suc a
# the other two spellings of the constructor, nested around a number and
# around a parenthesized expression
true: successor of 4 equals 5
true: the successor of (2 + 3) equals six
true: suc (suc (suc 3)) equals 6
false: suc (suc 2) equals 5

# ── questions: the same certificates, reported as answers ────────────────────
true: is two plus three five?
false: is two plus three six?
false: does two times three equal seven?
# `is ... equal to` and `does ... equal`, over a product and the constructor
true: is 42 equal to six times seven?
true: does the successor of three equal four?

# ── open claims: both sides rewrite to the same normal form ─────────────────
# (`for all a: a + suc b = suc (a + b)` and the rest of the recursion laws
# themselves are in `upstream.txt`, next to the MM0 theorems they come from.)
proved: for all x: x plus zero equals x
proved: for all x: x times zero equals zero
# parentheses around a whole side, the same claim as the first line here
proved: for all a: (a plus zero) equals a

# ── the honest negative: a true fact this fragment cannot prove ──────────────
# Commutativity needs induction; the theory has no induction rule, and the
# emitter does not guess. The expectation is "no verdict", so a future change
# that starts proving it must do so by adding a real rule to the theory.
unproved: for all x, y: x plus y equals y plus x

# ── kernel content: sentences about a numerical scheme, decided by citation ──
# `theory: kernel` switches to the fragment whose subject is a *symbolic kernel
# certificate* — the artifacts the unfer kernel's prover emits
# (`australVM/mm0/<stem>_certificate.mm0`), whose theorems state a scheme's
# obligations as polynomial identities over the state's components.
#
# The verdict is a citation, not a new proof: a sentence is `proved` when some
# theorem of that certificate states exactly the same identity, up to renaming
# the variables. Nothing here invents a proof term, and the certificate is only
# citable when the obligations sidecar it pins (`-- Source: … sha256 …`) is the
# one being read — so a drifted sidecar is refused rather than believed.
#
# These lines need `--kernel-certificate <path>`; without it the fragment is
# skipped with a note (this repo does not contain the certificate).
# `australVM/scripts/logos2mm0` always passes it, and checks the three verdict
# shapes below: cited, cited-under-hypothesis, and honestly unproved.
theory: kernel

# consistency — the numerical flux degenerates to the physical flux on a
# uniform state. `numerical flux` is the two-word spelling of `numFlux`; the
# equality word `agrees with` is admitted for this sentence's sake.
proved: for all u: numerical flux of u and u agrees with flux of u

# symmetry — the two-point operator is antisymmetric in its arguments
proved: for all l, r: delta of l and r plus delta of r and l equals zero

# conservation — the residuals telescope to zero around a periodic cycle
proved: for all a, b, c: delta of a and b plus delta of b and c plus delta of c and a equals zero

# the uniform interface: the one obligation with a hypothesis. The hypothesis
# is part of the identity (the certificate proves the statement on the locus
# `l = r`), so the `when` clause is what makes this sentence the theorem's.
proved: delta of l and r equals zero when l equals r

# ── the honest negative for this fragment ────────────────────────────────────
# A true property of the scheme that is not an identity over its state
# polynomials: order of accuracy is a statement about truncation error, not
# about ℤ[x⃗]. Nothing in the certificate states it, and the fragment does not
# dress a quality up as a theorem — the expectation is "no verdict", so this
# line starts failing the day something starts pretending otherwise.
unproved: the scheme is second order accurate
